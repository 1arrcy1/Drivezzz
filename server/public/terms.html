3. Drivezzz is a marketplace

Drivezzz brings riders and drivers together. Drivers set their own prices and offer rides themselves. The agreement for a ride is between the rider and the driver. We don't employ drivers, don't offer transport ourselves and aren't a party to that agreement.

  • We don't take responsibility for a driver's licences, permits or insurance. We do check what we can against the sources that exist, and a driver who passes gets a blue check. For a car in the Netherlands that means the driver's ID, face, chauffeurskaart, taxi permit and KvK registration, and the car's taxi registration, insurance and APK. A blue check is no guarantee: a document can be false or withdrawn without us seeing it, and anything can change after we checked. We don't screen riders. A "Checked" mark on a vehicle only means its plate was found in the Dutch vehicle register.
  • What the law asks for paid rides depends on where you drive. In some countries nothing is needed; in others a licence, permits and insurance (in the Netherlands, for example, a taxi licence from Kiwa, a chauffeurskaart and blue plates). As a driver you find out what applies where you drive, and you're responsible for having it and for a safe, road-legal vehicle. The app tells you so before you go online the first time.
  • You can use Drivezzz wherever the law allows it. You're responsible for following the law where you use it.

5. Rides

  • Drivers set their own rates. A quote includes the driver's way to you; on a price you name yourself, it doesn't.
  • You can have one order going at a time. A driver has 5 minutes to accept it; a driver who doesn't answer goes offline, and it goes to the next driver.
  • Ordering is free. Once a driver takes your ride you pay from your own wallet: within 5 minutes, or for a planned ride at the latest 30 minutes before the pickup. If it isn't paid in time the ride is cancelled and no money moves. A driver can already set off while you pay.
  • The driver has until a set time to reach the pickup: how long the way takes plus 30%, plus the 5 minutes for paying. The app shows that time.
  • Check in only when you're inside the vehicle, after checking the number plate. Being at the pickup isn't enough.
  • An order no driver takes ends after 2 hours, or for a planned ride at its time. Nothing was paid.
  • A driver checks out within 200 m of the drop-off. If your phone isn't within 500 m of it at that moment, you both get a heads-up. You can ignore it if you were dropped off correctly. If you didn't reach your destination, appeal within 24 hours.

7. Paying and money

  • Payment is in USDC or SOL on Solana, from your own wallet, or in cash to the driver. Prices are shown in euros. Drivezzz isn't a bank, a wallet or an exchange: it has no deposits, balances or withdrawals, doesn't exchange coins and doesn't hold your coins.
  • Each ride's payment waits in its own escrow, a program on the Solana blockchain. It can only go to the wallet that paid, the driver's wallet and Drivezzz's fee address.
  • Drivezzz picks the outcome under these terms: pay out, 50/50 or refund. A refund always goes to the wallet that paid. Drivezzz can also hold a ride's money while it checks something, or decide it early, also without an appeal. A hold can't outlast the refund after 30 days.
  • A driver is paid to their connected wallet 24 hours after check-out, unless someone appeals. A driver who doesn't reach the drop-off isn't paid.
  • You and the driver can each appeal within 24 hours after check-out. An appeal holds the money until a person at Drivezzz picks a refund, 50/50 or pay out. If a driver appeals after driving at least 60% of the way and nobody has decided within 3 days, the driver is paid.
  • If nothing is decided within 30 days of paying, the whole payment goes back to the wallet that paid. Anyone can start that on the blockchain, also if Drivezzz no longer exists.
  • The rider pays the network fees (about €0.001) and the escrow's deposit (about €0.10 to €0.25), which comes back by itself when the escrow closes.
  • Amounts are fixed in the coin when you pay. A refund returns that amount of coin, whatever it's worth by then. A planned ride paid in SOL pays that amount of SOL, whatever SOL is worth on the day.
  • A USDC payment can only reach a wallet that has a USDC account. If you close yours before a refund, the refund waits until it's back. A driver needs one to take USDC rides.
  • Crypto can lose value, and a payment on a blockchain can't be reversed. The escrow program has had no paid security audit, so a bug could lock or misroute money. Only pay what you can afford to lose.
  • If you choose cash, you pay the driver the whole price in euros, in the car. It's between you and the driver: Drivezzz never handles or checks cash, so nothing is held, refunded, split or paid out, and the ride shows as paid in cash once the driver has checked out. You can each still appeal within 24 hours, but Drivezzz can't move any money, only look at what happened.
  • Taxes on what you earn or pay in crypto or cash are your own responsibility.

What it costs

On a ride paid out in full, Drivezzz's fee of €0.10 comes out of your payout, in the ride's coin. There is no fee on a 50/50, a refund or a ride paid in cash, and there are no other costs.

How you're shown to riders

For a destination, drivers are listed by the price of the whole ride, cheapest first. With equal prices, the quickest pickup comes first for a ride now, and the most reviews for a planned ride. Before a destination is chosen, the list goes by price per km. Nobody can pay for a better place.

What the app decides by itself

  • Not answering an offer within 5 minutes puts you offline until you go online again.
  • Your on-time, completed and accepted percentages. Riders see the first two on your page.

A person at Drivezzz decides appeals, strikes and bans.

Complaints
